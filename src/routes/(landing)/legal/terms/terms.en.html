2. What {name} is

{name} is a free, invite-only climbing guidebook and logbook, and it has no public side.

You start a region, record crags, blocks and routes in it, draw topo lines over your photographs, log your ascents, and invite the people you climb with. A region is a closed container. Postgres row-level security (RLS) enforces the boundary: the database refuses to return rows from a region you were not invited to.

A region cannot be published, listed or opened to the open web. There is no public mode and there will not be one. An invite list can grow to hold more people, and the region still never becomes public.

{name} is free. There are no ads, no analytics, no tracking, no sale of data and no revenue. The source code is open under the MIT licence, and the repository is on GitHub for anybody to read. That licence covers the code. It gives nobody a licence to the content people post here, which clause 5 handles.

I owe you no particular availability. I run this on my own time, I change features, and parts of the service can be offline. If I shut the service down, I tell you at least 30 days before it happens, by email to the address on your account, and it stays readable for those 30 days so you can take out what matters to you. There is no export button. If you want your data as a file, ask me inside that window and section 8 of the privacy policy says how. A region admin closing their own region is their decision, not mine, so that notice is not mine to give.

A region holds up to {maxMembers} members by default, and an account can own up to {maxRegions} regions. Both are operational limits I set and can change, not promises I make to you, and both numbers come from the same values the app enforces.

The carve-out is route and topo data. Where other members logged an ascent against a route you created, the route entry stays in the region as shared route information, so their logbooks keep their meaning. That covers the entry itself: its name, its grade, its position and the topo lines drawn on the image. It does not cover your photographs, your videos or your comments.

Closing an account works like this, per kind of content. Your account and profile go: the email address, the display name, the sign-in records. Your ascent logs, your comments and the photographs and videos you uploaded go with them. Crag, block and route entries you created stay in the region under an anonymous author, because they are the guidebook other members are still using and their ascents hang off them. Once the author link is severed the entry is no longer personal data about you, so your erasure right under Article 17 GDPR is satisfied in full.

Deletion is the only way to take an entry out of a region. There is no visibility flag on an area, a block or a route, so I cannot hide one and keep it: I delete it or I leave it in place. Adding text to an entry is possible, and that is what a hazard warning is. Section 7 of the privacy policy covers retention periods and backups.
